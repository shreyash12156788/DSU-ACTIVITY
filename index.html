The "Bridge Hand" Intuition:

Imagine holding cards in your left hand. You pick up the next unsorted card with your right hand (the key), scan your left hand from right to left, shift cards that are greater to make room, and slide the key into its exact sorted position.

Viva Voce & Technical Defense Guide

Key examiner questions, trade-offs, and why Insertion Sort matters in production systems.

▼

Q1: Why study Insertion Sort if it's O(n²)?

Production Impact: Both Python's Timsort and standard C++ std::sort (Introsort) switch to Insertion Sort for subarrays with size ≤ 16 to 32 elements. Why? It has minimal constant factor overhead, zero recursion stack overhead, and exceptional CPU cache locality.

Q2: What does "Adaptive" mean here?

An algorithm is adaptive if its runtime improves when the input possesses existing order. Insertion sort achieves O(n) time on already-sorted or nearly-sorted data, performing only $n - 1$ comparisons and $0$ shifts.

Q3: Why is Insertion Sort Stable?

Because we use while A[j] > key (strictly greater). When an identical duplicate element is encountered, the comparison terminates immediately, placing the new key after the existing equal element, preserving original relative order.

Q4: How does it compare to Selection Sort & Bubble Sort?

Selection sort always takes $\Theta(n^2)$ comparisons even if already sorted. Bubble sort requires full swaps (3 writes). Insertion sort only performs shifts (1 write per movement), making it twice as fast in practice.
